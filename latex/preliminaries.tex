\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

This section fixes the notation and recalls the four established primitives
the framework composes. Each is stated in the canonical form of its source so
that Sections~\ref{sec:architecture}~and~\ref{sec:framework} can build on it
directly.

\subsection{Flow-Level Detection}
\label{subsec:flowdetection}

We address flow-level intrusion detection on IoT gateways. A flow
$\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ is a fixed-length vector of $d$ numeric
descriptors derived from a network connection. A detector is a map
\begin{equation}
  f : \mathbb{R}^{d} \to \mathcal{K},
  \qquad
  \mathcal{K} = \{c_1, \dots, c_K\},
  \label{eq:detector}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ is the space of flow vectors and $\mathcal{K}$ the
fixed set of $K$ known attack-family labels. The closed-set assumption that
every flow belongs to one of these $K$ families is standard but brittle, and
Section~\ref{subsec:zeroday} relaxes it for zero-day traffic. Detection
quality is reported as the macro-averaged $F_1$, the unweighted mean of the
per-class scores~\cite{sokolova2009metrics}
\begin{equation}
  \text{macro-}F_1 = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{c=1}^{K}
    \frac{2 \, P_c \, R_c}{P_c + R_c},
  \label{eq:macrof1}
\end{equation}
where $P_c$ and $R_c$ are the precision and recall of class $c$. The
unweighted mean weights every attack family equally regardless of its
prevalence, so a detector cannot hide poor minority-class performance behind
a dominant class.

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Generation}
\label{subsec:ragsynthesis}

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) conditions a generator on documents
pulled from an external store rather than on parametric memory
alone~\cite{lewis2020rag}. A retriever scores each document $z$ in a corpus
against a query $x$, the top-$k$ documents are taken as a latent variable,
and the output $y$ is marginalised over them,
\begin{equation}
  p(y \mid x) \approx
    \sum_{z \in \text{top-}k(p(\cdot \mid x))}
      p_\eta(z \mid x) \; p_\theta(y \mid x, z),
  \label{eq:rag}
\end{equation}
where $p_\eta(z \mid x)$ is the retriever's relevance score and
$p_\theta(y \mid x, z)$ is the generator conditioned on the retrieved
evidence~\cite{lewis2020rag}. The retriever scores documents by the cosine
similarity of dense embeddings~\cite{reimers2019sbert,song2020mpnet}. For an
embedding map $\phi$ and two flows $\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{sim}(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v})
  = \frac{\phi(\mathbf{u}) \cdot \phi(\mathbf{v})}
         {\lVert \phi(\mathbf{u}) \rVert \, \lVert \phi(\mathbf{v}) \rVert}
  \in [-1, 1],
  \label{eq:cosine}
\end{equation}
which is $1$ when the embeddings point the same way and $0$ when they are
orthogonal. We specialise this pattern so the corpus is the training split
of an IoT dataset and the generator emits a structured policy rather than
free text. The retriever and generator run only at the cloud or workstation
tier, touched once at offline policy-generation time, and no model weights or
embeddings are shipped to the edge.

\subsection{Feature Divergence and Importance}
\label{subsec:featurestats}

The generator is grounded in features that separate normal from attack
traffic. Two established statistics rank them. The standardised mean
difference, Cohen's $d$, measures how far apart two class means sit in
pooled-standard-deviation units~\cite{cohen1988power},
\begin{equation}
  d = \frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{s_{\text{pooled}}},
  \qquad
  s_{\text{pooled}} = \sqrt{\frac{s_1^2 + s_2^2}{2}},
  \label{eq:cohend}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{x}_i$ and $s_i$ are the mean and standard deviation of the
feature within class $i$. A large $|d|$ marks a feature whose value alone
separates the classes. Permutation importance complements this by measuring
the error increase when a feature is randomly shuffled~\cite{breiman2001randomforests},
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{VI}_j = \mathcal{L}\bigl(y, f(\mathbf{x}^{\pi_j})\bigr)
                - \mathcal{L}\bigl(y, f(\mathbf{x})\bigr),
  \label{eq:permimp}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x}^{\pi_j}$ permutes feature $j$ and $\mathcal{L}$ is the loss.
A feature with large $\mathrm{VI}_j$ is one the detector relies on. Cohen's
$d$ ranks the prompt context and $\mathrm{VI}_j$ ranks the features exposed
to the rule generator.

\subsection{Threshold-Rule Policies}
\label{subsec:thresholdpolicy}

The detector emitted by the generator is a policy of threshold rules. A
threshold rule is a tuple $r = (\,\text{feature},\, \text{op},\,
\text{value},\, c\,)$ that fires when the named feature of $\mathbf{x}$
satisfies the comparison and then votes for its target class $c$. For
example, a rule may fire when packet count exceeds a value and vote
\texttt{DDoS}. A policy is a set $\pi = \{r_1, \dots, r_n\}$ of such rules,
evaluated against $\mathbf{x}$ in a single vectorised comparison. The exact
weighted-vote aggregation that turns the fired rules into a label is the
paper's own construction and is defined in
Section~\ref{subsec:policy_generation}. Threshold rules are the same
primitive that IoT gateways already execute as access-control list (ACL)
firewall entries, so a generated policy deploys without new runtime
infrastructure. The compiled policy is the only artifact that reaches the
gateway. It runs at line rate within the power and memory budget of an edge
device, carries no model weights, and issues no language-model call per flow.
This offline-reasoning, edge-execution split is the computing-optimization
step that lets a large model harden an AIoT deployment without on-device
inference cost.

Section~\ref{sec:architecture} composes these primitives into the end-to-end
system, and Section~\ref{sec:framework} defines the four protocols that
exercise it.
