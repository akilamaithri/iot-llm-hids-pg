\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% Motivation: AIoT, edge LLMs, and the widened threat surface
The Artificial Intelligence of Things (AIoT) embeds learning directly into connected devices. It has turned passive sensor networks into systems that perceive, decide, and act with no human in the loop~\cite{chang2021aiot}. The same trend pulls foundation models and large language models (LLMs) toward the network edge, where their contextual reasoning promises richer perception and control~\cite{wang2025edgelam,frontiers2025edgellm}. Wider intelligence widens the attack surface. AIoT inherits the classical network threats of the IoT, including botnets and denial-of-service floods that recruit poorly defended devices at scale~\cite{antonakakis2017mirai}. It also inherits AI-specific threats such as adversarial perturbation and model poisoning that target the learning components themselves~\cite{rosenberg2021advml}. An intrusion detection system (IDS) for AIoT must hold accuracy as traffic shifts. At the same time, the solution must operate within the power, memory, and latency constraints of edge and fog deployment tiers.

% Why conventional IDS cannot resolve the three-way tension
Conventional signature- and anomaly-based detectors track new threats only through periodic retraining. That retraining is costly in energy and memory, and it scales poorly across the heterogeneous device populations of a real
deployment. Machine-learning and deep-learning detectors raise accuracy on every benchmark~\cite{ferrag2020dlids}. However, the most accurate models are the hardest to host on constrained hardware. Their opacity means a security practitioner cannot easily audit or modify a gradient-trained decision boundary, eroding the trust that operational deployment requires.~\cite{moustafa2023explainable}. Accuracy, deployability, and interpretability therefore pull against one another in every conventional design. Fig.~\ref{fig:problem} casts this as a pick-two constraint that no existing detector escapes.

% The reframing: large model as offline computing-optimization step
We resolve this tension by moving the large model out of the detection loop. It instead runs on the workstation or cloud tier, reasons over a representative sample of traffic and compiles a compact detection policy. The gateway then executes that policy in pure Python with no model weights and no inference-time accelerator. The heavy reasoning becomes a computing-optimization step at the cloud tier, and the edge runs a stateless evaluator. The split removes the communication, energy, and latency overhead that on-device large-model inference would impose. It preserves the interpretability that opaque classifiers cannot offer, because the compiled policy is a short list of human-readable threshold rules.

% How the design overcomes the known weaknesses of LLMs
A large model cannot be trusted to write detection rules from a bare prompt. It lacks intrinsic IoT-security expertise, hallucinates~\cite{huang2025hallucination}, and it reasons unreliably over long lists of numeric traffic~\cite{huang2024quantreasoning}. Our design grounds the model in evidence retrieved from a class-balanced corpus. For rule synthesis the model never sees raw flows. It receives a divergence-ranked statistical profile of the corpus, which bounds the token budget and anchors every threshold in measured data. A separate vector store of embedded labelled flows supplies nearest-neighbour class priors for multi-class labelling and the confidence
signal that drives zero-day detection. An agentic refinement loop then improves the policy over several rounds. A controlled-vocabulary prompt and a diversity-aware acceptance rule steer the model toward complementary detection phenomena and away from redundant rules. A weighted vote with a validation-calibrated threshold combines the accepted rules at inference time. The framework produces a high-precision policy at negligible offline cost, and its quality depends on the prompt structure rather than on any single model vendor.

% Objective and contributions
Hence, this work delivers a framework that compiles interpretable, semantic-aware intrusion-detection policies for AIoT through offline large-model reasoning. It optimizes jointly for detection accuracy, edge resource use, and operator trust. This paper makes the following four contributions.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{An offline large-model-driven policy-compilation paradigm
    for AIoT-IDS.} A retrieval-grounded agentic pipeline reasons over corpus evidence and emits threshold-based policies as natural-language-editable rules. This decouples the cost of large-model reasoning from the edge runtime. It also lets a security practitioner read and revise the deployed policy directly.

    \item \textbf{A system-level benchmark for edge-deployed policy synthesis across five IoT datasets.} The framework matches or approaches gradient-trained classifier performance across datasets, and we characterize the structural expressiveness limit where it falls short. We measure cross-vendor portability, synthesis cost, and compiled policy inference latency on Raspberry Pi hardware. Together these provide a reproducible performance profile for large-model-driven IDS at the edge.

    \item \textbf{A trustworthiness study under privacy and adversarial pressure.} We evaluate synthesized policies under feature-name anonymization to assess reliance on semantic priors, and under a Kerckhoffs threat model to assess adversarial hardness relative to tree-based baselines.

    \item \textbf{A retraining-free zero-day extension.} We add a retrieval-confidence protocol that extends the closed-set policy to unseen attack families without retraining, achieving strong detection rates while maintaining low false-positive rates. This lowers the operational burden of alarm management.
\end{itemize}
