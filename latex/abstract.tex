\begin{abstract}
The Artificial Intelligence of Things (AIoT) increasingly demands intelligent, adaptive security mechanisms capable of operating across resource-constrained and heterogeneous IoT infrastructures. Traditional intrusion detection systems (IDS) relying on pre-trained signature-based or anomaly-based models are often insufficient to handle evolving network-level threats and require frequent retraining, a process that is computationally prohibitive for edge IoT devices. 
While advanced machine learning and deep learning models offer improved detection accuracy, their deployment on resource-limited devices remains challenging, and current systems continue to suffer from high false positive rates that complicate alert reasoning. To address these limitations, this paper proposes a novel large language model (LLM)-driven framework that leverages advanced agentic design pattern and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) with vector databases for generating adaptive and interpretable IDS policies. By harnessing the contextual reasoning capabilities of large models, the framework dynamically generates security policies without requiring on-device model retraining, enabling efficient and scalable intrusion detection across distributed IoT environments. We evaluate the proposed approach on five real-world IDS datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in high-precision policy generation, achieving over 97\% precision on three datasets and over 80\% on two challenging ones. Notably, the framework reduces detection time by 50\% while utilizing only 5\% of the LLM context window for policy generation, highlighting its computational efficiency and suitability for large-model-driven AIoT deployments. Our findings establish a benchmark for LLM-based IoT-IDS and underscore the potential of intelligent computing optimization strategies for secure, efficient, and trustworthy large model integration in next-generation AIoT systems.
\end{abstract}
