\begin{abstract}
The Artificial Intelligence of Things (AIoT) increasingly depends on intelligent and adaptive security that runs across heterogeneous, resource-constrained edge infrastructures. Conventional Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) built on signature- or anomaly-based models require periodic retraining to track evolving threats. That computation is prohibitive on power- and memory-limited IoT devices. The deep models that raise accuracy are themselves hard to deploy at the edge and offer operators little interpretability. We argue that the right role for large language models (LLMs) in this setting is not on-device inference. It is offline, large-model-driven computing optimization. A one-time reasoning stage compiles a compact, edge-deployable detection policy. We present a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) agentic framework that synthesizes interpretable threshold policies for network intrusion detection. The framework uses no on-device retraining and no model weights at inference time. The heavy large-model computation runs on the workstation or cloud tier. The resulting JSON rule artifact executes at the gateway in pure Python. Across five IoT benchmarks (CIC-IoT2023, WUSTL-IIoT, TON\_IoT, Bot-IoT, UNSW-NB15), the framework attains macro-F1 $\geq 0.97$ on three datasets and $0.85$ and $0.70$ on the two structurally hardest. It matches gradient-trained classifiers on four of five datasets while remaining vendor-portable. Claude Haiku 4.5 and Gemini 2.5 Flash agree to within $0.012$~F1 on four datasets. The entire multi-seed policy-synthesis campaign costs \$3.30. At the edge the artifact infers in $0.044$--$0.184$\,ms per flow on Raspberry~Pi~4/5. This runs $144\times$--$163\times$ faster than Random Forest with negligible additional memory. The policies extend to withheld zero-day families without retraining and reach a detection rate $\geq 0.999$ on two datasets. They remain robust under feature-name anonymization and adversarial perturbation. The framework establishes a reproducible benchmark for trustworthy and efficient large-model-driven AIoT intrusion detection.
\end{abstract}
