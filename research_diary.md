## 📅 [2026-04-28 11:32] — Kerckhoffs Robustness: ESR vs. Perturbation Budget (All Conditions)

**Source(s):** `iot-llm-hids-pg/experiments/10-kerckhoffs-robustness.ipynb`, `experiments/results/kerckhoffs/kerckhoffs-results-2026-04-28-11-32-41.json`

### Summary
This experiment measures the adversarial evasion robustness of five IDS detection rule conditions — LLM_orig, LLM_anon, DT_d3, DT_d5, RF_d5 — across two IoT network datasets (CIC-IoT2023 withheld class: Mirai-udpplain; WUSTL-IIoT withheld class: Reconn). The core metric is the Evasion Success Rate (ESR) as a function of perturbation budget ε (in training std units), with the primary summary statistic being ε at ESR=0.5 (median evasion cost). All conditions are evaded at very small perturbation budgets (all ε@ESR=0.5 < 0.05), confirming that threshold-based IDS rules — whether LLM-generated or tree-learned — share a structural vulnerability to minimal feature perturbations. The most robust condition across both datasets is DT_d3 on WUSTL-IIoT (ε@ESR=0.5 = 0.03).

### Key Findings

**Median Evasion Cost (ε at ESR=0.5, std units):**

| Dataset     | LLM_orig | LLM_anon | DT_d3 | DT_d5 | RF_d5 |
|-------------|----------|----------|-------|-------|-------|
| CIC-IoT2023 | 0.008    | 0.002    | 0.005 | 0.005 | 0.008 |
| WUSTL-IIoT  | 0.001    | 0.020    | 0.030 | 0.001 | 0.001 |

*(Exact values from JSON: CIC-IoT LLM_orig = 0.007851, LLM_anon = 0.002113; WUSTL-IIoT LLM_anon = ~0.02, DT_d3 = ~0.03)*

- WUSTL-IIoT attack pool: 15,756 instances
  - LLM_orig: 11,548 flagged (73.3%) | LLM_anon: 11,929 (75.7%) | DT_d3: 15,747 (99.9%) | DT_d5: 15,755 (100.0%) | RF_d5: 15,748 (100.0%)

### Anomalies / Warnings
- ⚠️ **DT_d5 on WUSTL-IIoT is paradoxically fragile**: ε@ESR=0.5 = 0.00 (ESR=1.000 at ε=0.001), while the shallower DT_d3 achieves ε=0.03. The deeper tree creates more leaf paths with sharper, tighter thresholds that are individually cheap to violate — depth does not imply robustness for conjunctive path-based rules.
- ⚠️ **RF_d5 on WUSTL-IIoT also collapses early**: ESR=0.996 at ε=0.001, suggesting WUSTL-IIoT features have low std variance (small perturbation in raw units = large std-normalized perturbation).
- ⚠️ **LLM_anon detection rate is higher than LLM_orig on both datasets**, yet LLM_anon is less robust on CIC-IoT (ε=0.002 vs 0.008). Higher recall ≠ higher robustness; anonymization produces rules that fire more broadly but on less stable feature boundaries.
- ⚠️ **Protocol Type targeted only 2× for CIC-IoT LLM_orig** — this categorical feature is effectively unperturbable, consistent with the `inf` evasion cost logic in the code. Validates the std=0 guard.

### Notes for Paper
- 📝 **Methods section**: "We define the median evasion cost as the perturbation budget ε (in training standard deviation units) at which the Evasion Success Rate (ESR) reaches 0.5. An attacker is assumed to perturb feature values optimally under a ε-bounded budget; for LLM majority-vote rules, evasion requires flipping the majority of firing rules; for decision trees, evasion requires violating one condition in the matched attack-leaf path."
- 📝 **Results section**: "All five conditions are evadable with sub-0.05σ perturbations, confirming that threshold-based rules share a structural vulnerability irrespective of whether they were generated by an LLM or a classical learner. Notably, LLM_anon achieves the highest median robustness on WUSTL-IIoT (ε=0.02) and DT_d3 ranks second (ε=0.03), while DT_d5 — despite greater depth — is trivially evadable (ε≈0.001)."
- 📝 **Kerckhoffs framing**: This result supports a Kerckhoffs-principle argument: the security of these IDS rules should not rely on an adversary being unaware of the rule structure. Given the low evasion costs, the paper should frame this as motivating the anonymization approach — even if anonymization does not dramatically improve robustness, it raises the practical barrier by concealing feature semantics.
- 📝 **Table for paper**: Use the exact JSON values (not rounded) for the robustness table. The interpolated ε@ESR=0.5 (e.g., LLM_orig CIC-IoT = 0.0079) is more informative than the coarse grid values shown in Cell 6.
